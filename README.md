# Why are we here?

The course assumes I'm either a designer or an artist. I'm actually somewhere in between; I'm a developer (more specifically, I have a B.Sc. in Video Game Programming). As a lead developer on a small indie company, I have worn multiple hats, handling marketing, project management, programming, art, and technical leadership.

Although I do touch art, design, sound, project management, and other areas of development, my main role has been programming. With AI becoming more common, this has been increasingly under the microscope. While some may have been skeptical about AI a year ago, they will soon discover (if they haven't already) that generative AI is not going anywhere; it is here to stay.

It is a bizarre thing to think about: imagine entering a painting class only to find out you can rely on AI entirely to paint. No brush is needed, and you don't get your hands dirty.

At the same time, it is almost impossible not to rely on AI for coding, so <b>finding a balance between recklessly vibecoding and using AI as a tool is what I hope to learn from this course. To my understanding, it is not about learning how to make a hammer, but about learning how to use it properly in real-world scenarios.</b> You need to:

1. Know what you're asking for. You can't just ask a clanker to paint a masterpiece. What shape, what style, how many? This is where your creativity comes into play.
2. Be able to read the output. In the real world, you won't be working on small class projects or game jams, and you will soon discover that vibecoding a few-week product will make you little to no profit at all. You will end up with dozens of repositories with no real market. What you will end up doing instead is writing (or prompting) maintainable code that you and your teammates can read months from now. Why would a company hire you? They can just replace you with an AI agent. You have to learn to use the environment, read code, fix bugs, and identify weak points.
3. Understand that times are changing. After all, programming languages are a way of communicating with the machine. In this case, we are at the highest level possible, using natural English language. Failing to understand this will leave your competition light years ahead of you.
4. Do not oversimplify things. With the industrial revolution, many jobs were eliminated, but this created different jobs. Your efforts were redirected elsewhere. Unfortunately, this is no longer the case; the new wave of AI has brought a new wave of layoffs. Knowing where to put your efforts is very important.
5. Take pride in your creation. As controversial as it sounds, you were the one who prompted it, so the creation is yours. On the same note, know when to get off your high horse: if two people can give the same prompt, what makes your creation better than the rest?
