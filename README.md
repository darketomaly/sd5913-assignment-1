# Why are we here?

The course assumes I'm either a designer or an artist. I'm actually somewhere inbetween, I'm a developer (more specifically, I have a B.Sc. in Video Game Programming). As a lead developer on a small indie company, I have worn multiple hats, handling marketing, project management, programming, art and technical leadership.

Though I do touch art, design, sound, project management and other areas of development, my main role has been programming. With AI nowadays, this has been increasingly under the microscope. While some may be skeptical about AI a year ago, they will soon find out (if they haven't yet) that generative AI is not going anywhere, it's here to stay.

It's a bizarre thing to think about - imagine entering a painting class, only to find out you can rely on AI entirely to paint. No brush needed or getting you hands dirty.

At the same time, it's almost impossible not to rely on AI for coding; so finding a balance between recklessly vibecoding and using AI as a tool is what I hope to learn from this course. To my understanding, it's not learning how to make a hammer, but learning how to properly use it with real-world scenarios. You need to:
1. Know what you're asking for. You can't just ask a clanker to paint a masterpiece. What shape, what style, how many - this is where your creativity takes part in.
2. Be able to read the output. In the real world, you won't be working on small class projects or game jams, and you will find out soon that vibecoding a few-week product will make you little to no profit at all. You will end up with dozens of repositories with no real market. What you will end up doing instead, is writing (or prompting) maintainable code that you and your teammates can read months from now. Why would a company hire you if you don't know how to read code? They can just replace you with an AI agent. You have to learn to use the environment, read code, fix bugs and identify weak points.
3. Understand that times are changing. Linus once said that after all, programming languages are a way of communicating with the machine, in this case, we are not on the highest level possible, using natural english language. Not understanding this, will end up in your competition being light years ahead of you.
4. Do not oversimplify things. With the industrial revolution, a lot of jobs were killed. But this created different jobs. Your efforts were put into something else. Unfortunately, this is no longer the case, the new wave of AI brought a new wave of layoffs. Knowing where to put your efforts is very important.
5. Take pride of your creation. As controversial as it sounds, you were the one who prompted something, so the creation is yours. On the same way, know when to get off your high horse; if two persons can give the same prompt, what makes your creation better than the rest?
